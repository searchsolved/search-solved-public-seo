# How to look after your running shoes

A good pair of running shoes should last between 300 and 500 miles. How you treat them between runs decides which end of that range you reach. These habits take a few minutes and add months to the life of a pair.

## Dry them properly

After a wet run, take out the insoles and loosen the laces. Stuff the shoes with newspaper and leave them at room temperature. Do not put them on a radiator or in a tumble dryer, because heat hardens the midsole foam and weakens the glue that holds the sole on. Change the newspaper after an hour if the shoes are soaked.

## Clean them by hand

Brush off dry mud with a soft brush. For anything that remains, use lukewarm water, a little mild soap and an old toothbrush. Washing machines are hard on running shoes: the drum bends the heel counter and the detergent strips the water resistant coating from trail running shoes. Rinse trail shoes after every muddy run so that grit does not wear through the stitching.

## Rotate two pairs

Midsole foam needs about a day to recover its shape after a run. If you run most days, alternate between two pairs. Both pairs will last longer, and many runners find that a second pair with a different fit also reduces niggles. A gait analysis at a running shop will tell you which shoes suit the way you run.

## Use them only for running

Wearing running shoes to the gym, in the garden or for the school run adds miles that do not show in your training log. Keep an old pair for everyday wear. Wear proper running socks too: they reduce the friction that wears out the heel lining.

## Know when to replace them

Log the miles for each pair in your running app. Look for creases along the side of the midsole, outsole tread worn smooth under the ball of the foot, and a heel that leans to one side when the shoe stands on a table. New aches in the knees or shins after easy runs are often the first sign that the cushioning has gone.

## Store them well

Keep shoes somewhere cool and dry, away from direct sunlight. A car boot in summer gets hot enough to damage the foam.
